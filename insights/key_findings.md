# Risk Ranking Findings

## 1. High-Risk Projects Exist Across Multiple Departments

The risk ranking identified projects with maximum risk scores of 25, representing the highest risk exposure within the dataset.

High-risk projects were identified across Finance, IT, Operations, Marketing, HR and Procurement.

This indicates that risk exposure is not isolated to a single business function.

## 2. Several Projects Require Increased PMO Attention

Projects including PRJ048, PRJ014, PRJ066, PRJ012, PRJ064, PRJ053, PRJ076, PRJ112 and PRJ101 recorded maximum risk scores of 25.

These projects should be prioritised for PMO review, particularly where high risk is combined with delays, cost overruns or significant issues.

## 3. Maximum Risk Score Does Not Tell the Complete Story

Projects can share the same maximum risk score while having different overall risk profiles.

| Project | Maximum Risk Score | Average Risk Score |
| PRJ014 | 25 | 25.00 |
| PRJ101 | 25 | 13.25 |
| PRJ066 | 25 | 18.33 |
| PRJ053 | 25 | 15.00 |

PRJ014 demonstrates consistently high risk exposure, whereas PRJ101 has a lower average risk despite having one risk reaching the maximum score.

This shows why both maximum and average risk scores should be considered when prioritising projects.

## 4. Risk Ranking Provides a Practical PMO Prioritisation Method

Using `RANK()` and `ROW_NUMBER()` allows projects to be ranked within their respective departments.

This provides the PMO with a structured way to identify the highest-risk projects in each business area and focus governance, monitoring and escalation resources where they are most needed.

## 5. Risk Should Be Considered Alongside Wider Project Performance
